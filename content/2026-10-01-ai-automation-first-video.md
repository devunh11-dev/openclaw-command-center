## Script draft
Hook:
Most people trying to automate YouTube start with the tools. That's backwards. The real automation is the pipeline.

Today we're building the pipeline: research, decide, write, create, review, publish, measure, and feed the results back into the next video.

The first layer is research. Use search and creator intelligence to find what people are actually discussing. The important part is keeping the source links with the idea so the script can be checked later.

Second is the decision gate. An agent can generate ten ideas, but the channel still needs a clear promise. We only move one approved idea into production.

Third is scripting. The AI gets the research, the channel voice, the target viewer, and the exact promise. It produces a draft—not an automatic publish.

Fourth is production. Voiceover, B-roll, graphics, captions, and thumbnail generation can be delegated to specialized tools. The orchestrator passes the approved assets between them.

Fifth is quality control. Check facts, sources, copyright/asset rights, pacing, the first few seconds, and whether the finished video actually delivers the promise in the title.

Then comes publishing. Metadata is generated from the approved script, but the final upload is still an explicit approval step.

After publication, analytics become new input. Views, watch time, retention, click-through rate, comments, and traffic sources tell us what happened. We don't guess. We record the observed result and use it to improve the next brief.

That's the difference between a pile of AI tools and a content machine: every stage has an input, an output, a verification step, and a handoff.

The goal isn't to remove the human. It's to remove the repetitive work around the human.

CTA:
If you want the exact stack and workflow, the next video will break down the automation layer step by step.

## Production checklist
- [ ] Verify every factual claim against primary/current sources before recording.
- [ ] Generate/approve thumbnail.
- [ ] Generate voiceover.
- [ ] Assemble video.
- [ ] Review full video.
- [ ] Approve title + description + thumbnail.
- [ ] Publish only after explicit approval.
- [ ] Record resulting YouTube URL and metrics.
